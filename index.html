Marketing that starts with your listing’s metadata.

Before ads or social posts, a book needs a page that Amazon search can place and a reader can trust. Metadata optimization sets up the three things Amazon uses to decide where your book shows.

What is metadata? It is everything on your listing that tells Amazon what the book is and who it is for: the title fields, description, keywords and categories. Amazon uses it to decide which shoppers see your book. Shoppers use the same page to decide whether to buy. If either part is weak, every ad and social post sends readers to a page that does not convert.

Description

The description is the sales page inside your listing.

  • Rewritten around the reader: who the book is for, what it promises and why they should care.
  • Formatted for skimming, with a strong opening line, short paragraphs and clear emphasis.
  • Your main search phrases worked in naturally, so it helps the listing rank without sounding stuffed.

Why it matters: this is where a shopper who found your book decides to buy it.

Keyword research

Keywords are the phrases readers type into Amazon’s search bar.

  • Research into what readers in your genre search for, and what comparable books are found for.
  • The strongest phrases placed in your title fields and the seven backend keyword slots.
  • A focus on phrases your book can realistically rank for, not only the biggest, most crowded terms.

Why it matters: a book matched to the wrong searches never reaches the readers who would buy it.

Categories

Categories decide which bestseller lists and browse pages your book appears on.

  • Categories chosen for fit with your content and a realistic chance of reaching the top of the list.
  • Overcrowded categories avoided when a more focused one suits the book better.
  • A listing placed where your readers actually browse.

Why it matters: a high rank in a focused category is visible on your listing and puts the book in front of browsing readers.

How it works

  1. AuditI review your listing: title fields, description, keywords, categories, cover and reviews.
  2. ResearchKeyword and category research for your genre and your target reader.
  3. RewriteA new description, a keyword set and a category plan, ready to put on your listing.

FAQ

Questions authors ask.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the project. The price for metadata optimization depends on your book, your genre and how much of the listing needs work. Request a quote with your listing link and I will price it from there.

What does metadata optimization include?

Your book description, keyword research and category selection. Together they decide where your book appears in Amazon search and on bestseller lists, and how clearly the page speaks to a shopper.

Can you guarantee my book will rank or sell?

No. Rank moves with sales and reviews as well as metadata. In my case study the rank improved a lot, and reviews grew in the same period. I will tell you what I would change and what to expect, and I will not promise a number.

What do I need to send?

Your Amazon listing link, or your draft if the book is not published yet. Add your genre, your target reader and anything you have already tried, such as ads, price changes or a new cover.

Do you also edit, format and design covers?

Yes. Marketing is the main service, and editing, formatting, cover design, A+ Content and book trailers cover the rest of the journey from draft to published. Each is quoted by project.

Which platforms do you work with?

Metadata optimization is built around Amazon. Formatting and cover design also suit IngramSpark, Barnes & Noble Press, Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books, Lulu and Draft2Digital.

How do I contact you?

Use the quote form on this page, or tap the WhatsApp button at the corner of the screen. Both reach me directly.

Will you buy or write reviews for my book?

No. Reviews should come from real readers. Amazon prohibits paid and fake reviews, and they erode the trust a listing depends on.
